Essential Astronomy Glossary

Bortle Scale
A nine-level numeric scale measuring the darkness of a night sky, crucial for planning deep-sky observation. Lower numbers (1-3) mean darker skies.
Visual Magnitude (Mag)
A logarithmic scale used to measure the brightness of an astronomical object as seen by the human eye. **Lower numbers mean brighter objects** (e.g., the Sun is -26.7; the faintest visible star is +6).
Deep-Sky Object (DSO)
An astronomical object that is not an individual star or a solar system object (e.g., planets, comets). Includes galaxies, nebulae, and star clusters.
Zenith
The point in the sky or celestial sphere directly above an observer, corresponding to an altitude of 90 degrees.

Guides & Equipment Advice

Choosing Your First Telescope

A simple guide to navigating the world of refractors, reflectors, and catadioptrics. Focus on aperture, portability, and mount type.

  • **Aperture is King:** The diameter of the main light-gathering lens or mirror (usually measured in millimeters or inches) is the most critical factor.
  • **Know Your Mount:** Alt-Azimuth (simple movement) vs. Equatorial (tracking stars) mounts.
  • **Start Simple:** For beginners, a Dobsonian reflector or a small refractor is often recommended for their ease of use.
